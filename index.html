⚠️ War & Geopolitics
2026 Iran War — Tankers Move Through Hormuz; Geneva Signing June 19
The deal is now physically taking hold. On June 17 the first Iranian oil tankers left the Strait of Hormuz since the US naval blockade began (April 13), after Trump authorized reopening the Strait and ending the blockade. The formal US-Iran peace treaty is set to be signed Friday, June 19 in Geneva, brokered partly via Pakistan PM Sharif and timed after the G7 summit. Terms extend the ceasefire 60 days, lift dueling Hormuz blockades, and halt Israel-Hezbollah fighting — but Iran’s nuclear program is punted to a later round. The war began Feb 28, 2026 (US/Israeli strikes killed Supreme Leader Khamenei; Iran closed Hormuz). Brent has collapsed from a ~$118–120 March peak to ~$79 on June 17.

So what: This is removing the single biggest oil-supply risk of 2026, but normalization will take weeks (hundreds of ships still repositioning in the Gulf). The June 19 signing is a binary catalyst — a slip or a nuclear-track collapse snaps the war premium back. Investment angle: Energy tail-risk hedges are unwinding and non-Gulf producers lose their scarcity premium; watch reconstruction and sanctions-relief plays if the signing holds.

Russia-Ukraine — Ukraine Arrests Russian Momentum; Peace Plan Reworked to 20 Points
Ukraine has arrested much of the momentum of Russia’s spring-summer offensive across the ~1,200km front. Sumy sees persistent attacks but no operational breakthrough toward the city; Pokrovsk stays fiercely contested with Russia unable to convert tactical gains. Overnight June 16, Russia struck with 132 UAVs; Ukraine reported ~1,460 Russian troop losses in 24 hours. Diplomatically, the original Russia-favorable 28-point plan (Witkoff-Dmitriev) has been reworked into a 20-point framework requiring Russian withdrawal from occupied parts of Dnipropetrovsk, Mykolaiv, Sumy and Kharkiv — still stalled after three rounds of talks (UAE/Switzerland, Jan–Feb 2026).

So what: The battlefield is hardening into stalemate while the diplomatic track stays frozen; neither side has capacity for a decisive breakthrough. Investment angle: European defense spending (2.5%+ GDP) remains locked in for a decade; drone/counter-drone stays the fastest-growing segment. Ukraine reconstruction ($500B+) activates only on a genuine settlement — not yet in sight, and offering no relief trade like Iran.

📈 Economy & Trade
US Inflation — May CPI Spikes to 4.2%, a 3-Year High
May CPI (released June 10) hit 4.2% YoY, the highest in three years, with headline up 0.5% MoM. Energy drove 60%+ of the gain — gasoline rose 40.5% YoY off the Iran-war oil shock. Core was far softer at 2.9% YoY / +0.2% MoM, signaling this is a war-driven supply spike rather than broad reflation. With Brent now falling on the Iran ceasefire and gas prices easing in June, May likely marks the inflation peak.

So what: The data is backward-looking energy pain; the forward path hinges entirely on whether the Hormuz reopening sticks. Investment angle: If May is the peak, disinflation resumes into H2 and rate-cut expectations can rebuild — supportive for duration and rate-sensitive equities.

Central Banks — Fed Holds but Turns Hawkish; Dots Now Signal a 2026 HIKE
The June 17 FOMC held 12-0 at 3.50–3.75% (4th straight hold) — but the dot plot turned sharply hawkish in Kevin Warsh’s debut as Chair. The 2026 inflation outlook was hiked to 3.6% headline / 3.3% core (from 2.7% in March), and the median year-end funds rate rose to 3.8% (from 3.4%), implying at least one HIKE this year; 9 of 18 members project a hike, 6 see two. Separately, the ECB hiked to 2.25% on June 11 (first hike since 2023) to counter war-driven inflation (eurozone CPI 3.2%), cutting 2026 GDP to 0.8%.

So what: This is a genuinely hawkish surprise — the Fed is now signaling hikes, not cuts, even as growth slows. Textbook stagflation policy bind. The Iran de-escalation and falling oil are the only clean off-ramp. Investment angle: Bullish USD, bearish duration/long bonds, headwind for rate-sensitive growth and small caps; watch the market-vs-Fed gap close toward the dots.
