Iran — Ceasefire Collapses: Trump Declares Truce “Over” at NATO Summit After US Hits 80+ Targets and Iran Strikes 85 US Sites in Bahrain & Kuwait
The US–Iran truce broke down hard. On July 8, at the NATO summit in Ankara, President Trump declared the ceasefire “over” — roughly three weeks after the June MoU to halt fighting, lift the US naval blockade and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The trigger: attacks on three commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz on July 6–7, after which US CENTCOM launched “powerful strikes” on 80+ targets; Iran’s IRGC responded by hitting 85 US military sites in Bahrain and Kuwait. Both sides traded dozens more strikes for a second straight day. Trump threatened to resume the naval blockade, launch more strikes and “take over” Kharg Island, but a day later signaled the exchange “would not lead to long-term military action.” Core dispute: Iran wants to control Hormuz transit and collect fees; the US rejects it.

So what: The biggest reversal since last briefing — a fragile ceasefire is now an active shooting exchange, re-injecting a war premium into oil. Investment angle: Watch Brent for a Hormuz spike above $70–72; defense, tankers/shipping-insurance and energy volatility favored; reconstruction plays pushed further out.

Trade — USMCA Left Unrenewed (July 1) as North American Pact Enters a Decade of Annual Reviews; US–EU Deal Live at 15% Tariff
The Trump administration declined to renew the ~$2T USMCA on July 1, opening a decade of annual reviews; absent a renewal, the pact expires July 2036. Goods that traded tariff-free keep doing so, but US tariffs remain on autos, industrial metals, wood and non-USMCA-origin Canadian/Mexican goods. Washington meets Mexico the week of July 20 for a mostly bilateral round; Canada trails. The US–EU deal went live July 1 (EU drops duties on US industrial goods; most EU exports face a 15% US tariff), and the US floated a 100% tariff on any country levying a digital-services tax. Some tariffs still face court challenges on legality.

So what: The last stabilizing pillar of North American trade is now a 10-year open negotiation; tariff walls harden region by region. Investment angle: Reinforces friend-shoring and pricing-power compounders; import-exposed autos/ag face prolonged uncertainty.

Global Inflation Ticks Up to 4.7% for 2026; Growth Slows as Tariffs and Fragmentation Bite
Global headline inflation is set to rise from 4.1% (2025) to 4.7% (2026) before easing to 3.9% in 2027 (IMF), keeping central banks cautious. Growth is cooling toward ~2.5–2.9% as tariffs, energy shocks and supply-side disruption raise costs (Deloitte/EY/World Bank). Much of the tariff-driven price pass-through still lies ahead — firms have run down pre-tariff inventories — while AI-related investment in North America and Asia provides a partial offset.

So what: A slower, higher-cost, more fragmented expansion with sticky prices. Investment angle: Favor pricing-power compounders, domestic supply chains, real assets and AI capex beneficiaries over rate-sensitive, import-exposed names.
