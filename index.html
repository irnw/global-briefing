⚠️ War & Geopolitics
Iran War — Strait of Hormuz Still Blocked
US-Israel air campaign (Operation Epic Fury) concluded May 5 but the Strait of Hormuz remains blocked since Feb 28. Iran presented a 14-point response to US peace proposals. Oil supply down 12.8 mb/d since Feb; Brent swinging $100–$144/bbl, currently ~$110.

So what: The Hormuz blockade is the single largest macro variable right now. Every day it persists, import-dependent economies (Japan, India, South Korea, EU) burn through strategic reserves. Resolution unlocks a deflationary tailwind; prolonged closure forces central banks into impossible rate decisions — tighten into energy inflation or ease into stagflation. Investment angle: Long energy security plays (LNG infrastructure, non-Gulf producers like Guyana/Brazil deepwater). Short consumer discretionary in oil-importing economies. Watch Iran’s 14-point response closely — any framework acceptance triggers a massive risk-on rally in energy importers.

Investment angle: European defense names (Rheinmetall, BAE) remain strong regardless of peace talks — the spending commitments are locked in for years. EU natural gas futures stay bid through winter. Reconstruction plays (building materials, grid equipment) are a medium-term thesis once ceasefire odds improve.

📈 Economy & Trade
Oil Shock — Worst Since 1970s
Global oil supply fell to 95.1 mb/d. IEA projects Q2 demand contraction of 1.5 mb/d — sharpest since COVID. Gulf output 14.4 mb/d below pre-war levels. Strategic reserves deployed but insufficient.

So what: This isn’t a temporary supply disruption — it’s a structural repricing of energy risk. Unlike COVID, demand destruction here is involuntary (price-driven, not lockdown-driven), meaning recovery snaps back violently once supply normalizes. The strategic reserve drawdowns buy 3–6 months at most. Investment angle: Energy equities (especially non-Gulf producers) are a hedge, but the real opportunity is in the second-order effects: petrochemical input costs crushing margins for industrials, airlines, and logistics companies. Short those sectors in oil-importing economies. Long alternative energy infrastructure — this crisis makes every government energy-independence proposal fundable.
CRITICALINFLATION
Eurozone Growth Cut to 0.9%
EU Commission slashed GDP forecast from prior 1.4% expectation. Inflation forecast raised to 3% (from 1.9%). Energy shock + trade volatility driving downgrades.

So what: The Eurozone is walking into a stagflationary trap. The ECB cut rates prematurely into the oil shock, and now faces a credibility problem — inflation re-accelerating while growth stalls. This divergence from the Fed (which paused) weakens the euro, making energy imports even more expensive in a vicious feedback loop. Investment angle: European equities look cheap on multiples but the earnings downgrades haven’t fully hit yet. Avoid cyclicals. European defense and energy security names are the exception — they benefit from both fiscal expansion and geopolitical urgency. EUR/USD likely drifts lower until Hormuz resolves.
RECESSION RISK
China Activity Weakening
Industrial output +4.1% YoY (down from 5.7%). Retail sales +0.2% YoY — weakest since late 2022. China no longer buys significant US exports.

So what: The headline numbers mask a dangerous divergence: industrial output is slowing because export demand is weakening (trade fragmentation), while domestic consumption is near-zero because the property wealth effect is still negative. Beijing’s stimulus has propped up supply-side (manufacturing, infrastructure) but hasn’t fixed the demand problem. This means China is exporting deflation globally — overcapacity in EVs, solar, steel gets dumped into world markets, compressing margins for competitors everywhere. Investment angle: Avoid China consumer plays until retail sales sustainably exceed 3% YoY. The global deflationary pressure from Chinese overcapacity benefits buyers (e.g., SEA manufacturers sourcing cheap Chinese inputs) and hurts competitors (EU/US steel, solar manufacturers facing dumping).
DEMAND WEAK
US-China Trade — Tariff Truce Expiring
One-year trade truce (Oct 2025) nearing expiration with no extension agreed. Section 301 investigations concluding this summer. 178 product exclusions extended to Nov 2026. Bessent signals China may accept restored tariff rates.

So what: The truce expiry is a binary event. If tariffs snap back to 60%+ levels, it’s an immediate hit to US consumer prices (especially electronics, apparel) and Chinese export volumes. But Bessent’s signaling suggests both sides want a deal — the question is whether domestic politics in both countries allow it. The 178 exclusions extension is telling: Washington is quietly protecting its own supply chains while talking tough. Investment angle: Companies with diversified manufacturing (Vietnam, India, Mexico nearshoring) outperform regardless of outcome. Pure China-export-dependent names carry binary risk. Watch for a deal announcement as a catalyst for EM and commodity recovery.

GROWTHAI HARDWARE
Bottleneck: HBM & Advanced Packaging
The real supply constraint isn’t logic capacity — it’s HBM and 2.5D/3D packaging throughput. These cap total AI GPU/accelerator shipments regardless of fab capacity.

So what: This is the most underappreciated bottleneck in the AI supply chain. TSMC can build all the 3nm wafers it wants, but if SK Hynix and Micron can’t produce enough HBM3E/HBM4, and if CoWoS advanced packaging capacity is maxed, the chips can’t ship. This creates a pricing power dynamic for packaging and memory that persists even as logic capacity expands. Investment angle: SK Hynix trades at a fraction of NVIDIA’s multiple despite being equally essential to AI chip production. Advanced packaging equipment makers (Disco, ASM Pacific, Besi) have order backlogs extending into 2028. These are the constraint owners — they set the ceiling on the entire AI hardware buildout.

🌎 Emerging World Trends
1
Multipolar Fragmentation Accelerating
Nations pursuing self-sufficiency in energy, minerals, and tech. US-China decoupling deepening. Iran war reshaping alliances. Defense spending rising across EU, Asia, Middle East.

Implication: The era of optimizing for efficiency (just-in-time, single-source supply chains) is giving way to optimizing for resilience (redundancy, nearshoring, strategic stockpiles). This structurally raises costs across every global supply chain but creates massive opportunities in logistics infrastructure, critical minerals extraction, and domestic manufacturing capacity. The investment playbook shifts from “who’s cheapest?” to “who’s most secure?”
2
AI Supercycle Entering Infrastructure Phase
Beyond model training — now about compute buildout, energy demand, agent infra, and custom silicon. $45B+ NVIDIA capex. 10 GW OpenAI accelerator deal.

Implication: AI has shifted from “will this work?” to “can we build it fast enough?” The bottleneck is now physical infrastructure: power generation, cooling, fiber optic connectivity, and advanced packaging capacity. This means the value chain is broadening — the next wave of AI wealth creation flows to power utilities, industrial REITs near data center hubs, and construction/engineering firms. The pure-software AI plays are increasingly priced; the infrastructure plays still have runway.
3
Energy Security as Geopolitical Imperative
Hormuz crisis turned energy independence from aspiration to survival. Nations stockpiling, diversifying, reshoring energy. Oil volatility becoming structural, not cyclical.

Implication: Energy security spending will persist regardless of how the Hormuz situation resolves — the political imperative is now irreversible. This creates a multi-decade capex cycle in LNG, nuclear, grid modernization, and renewables+storage. The counterintuitive play: fossil fuel demand doesn’t decline as fast as climate models project, because the transition requires building parallel systems before retiring old ones. Energy is the new defense spending — politically untouchable and structurally growing.
4
Physical AI Goes Mainstream
Foundation-model progress entering the physical world: humanoid robots, autonomous construction, warehouse automation. China leading volume, US leading valuation. $5.3B VC in one month.

Implication: The US-China split here is significant. China is mass-producing humanoid robots at price points ($16–20K) that make them viable for factory deployment today. The US focuses on higher-value, harder problems (construction, hazardous environments) at premium prices. The convergence point — when Chinese cost structures meet US capability levels — is likely 2028–2030 and will be a labor market inflection point comparable to the smartphone revolution for consumer tech.
5
Stagflation Risk in Import-Dependent Economies
Oil shock + trade fragmentation + weakening China demand. Eurozone growth halved, inflation doubled. Echoes of 1970s macro environment.

Implication: Stagflation is the one macro environment that punishes both traditional stock and bond portfolios. The 1970s playbook that worked: real assets (commodities, infrastructure, gold), dividend aristocrats with pricing power, and floating-rate debt. What didn’t work: growth equities without cash flow, long-duration bonds, and leveraged balance sheets. If this environment persists through 2026, portfolio construction matters more than stock picking.
