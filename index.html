⚠️ War & Geopolitics
2026 Iran War — War ENDS: US-Iran Deal Reopens Hormuz
The 2026 Iran-Israel war is ending. On June 13–14 the US and Iran confirmed a deal to halt the war that began Feb 28, 2026 (when US-Israeli strikes killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and Iran retaliated by closing the Strait of Hormuz). Trump authorized a toll-free reopening of Hormuz and immediate removal of the US naval blockade, with a signing ceremony set for Friday in Switzerland. Through the conflict the US drained ~58 million barrels (14%) from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve to cushion the closure. Brent fell more than 4% on the news to below $86.5 (June 12), from ~$97 on June 5.

So what: This removes the single biggest oil-supply risk of 2026 — the chokehold that drove May CPI to a 3-year high and forced the ECB’s first hike since 2023. The key question now is durability: a held ceasefire means oil/inflation relief, while any relapse re-arms the entire stagflation thesis. Investment angle: Energy tail-risk hedges that looked underpriced are now unwinding; non-Gulf producers lose their scarcity premium. Watch reconstruction and sanctions-relief plays if the deal holds.

Russia-Ukraine — Russia Accelerates in Sumy as 28-Point Peace Plan Stalls
Russian gains sped up through June, taking several villages in Sumy Oblast and advancing to within ~20km of the regional capital, though no operational breakthrough toward Sumy city has materialized. The Pokrovsk sector remains fiercely contested and the Kramatorsk-Kostiantynivka belt is now the principal theater. Diplomatically, a US-brokered 28-point peace plan (Witkoff-Dmitriev) has stalled over territorial concessions Kyiv and European allies reject as favoring Moscow’s maximalist demands. Three rounds of talks (UAE/Switzerland, Jan–Feb 2026) produced no breakthrough; March talks were postponed by the Iran war.

So what: Battlefield momentum and the diplomatic track continue to diverge — Russia is negotiating from a position of grinding strength while the peace framework is seen as one-sided. Investment angle: European defense spending (2.5%+ GDP) remains locked in for a decade; drone/counter-drone stays the fastest-growing segment. Ukraine reconstruction ($500B+) activates on any genuine settlement — not yet in sight.

📈 Economy & Trade
US Inflation — May CPI Spikes to 4.2%, a 3-Year High
May CPI (released June 10) hit 4.2% YoY, the highest in three years, with headline up 0.5% MoM. Energy drove 60%+ of the gain — gasoline rose 40.5% YoY off the Iran-war oil shock. Core was far softer at 2.9% YoY / +0.2% MoM, signaling this is a war-driven supply spike rather than broad reflation. With Brent now falling on the Iran ceasefire and gas prices easing in June, May likely marks the inflation peak.

So what: The data is backward-looking energy pain; the forward path hinges entirely on whether the Hormuz reopening sticks. Investment angle: If May is the peak, disinflation resumes into H2 and rate-cut expectations can rebuild — supportive for duration and rate-sensitive equities.

Central Banks — Fed on Hold, ECB Hikes for First Time Since 2023
The FOMC meets June 16–17 with markets pricing ~99.5% odds of NO change, holding the fed funds rate at 3.5–3.75%; the Fed has dropped any easing bias and is open to hikes if inflation persists. The ECB hiked to 2.25% on June 11 — its first hike since 2023 — a quarter-point move to counter war-driven energy inflation (eurozone CPI 3.2% in May), and cut its 2026 GDP forecast to 0.8%. So what: Major central banks turned hawkish on a supply shock just as growth slowed — textbook stagflation-lite. The Iran ceasefire is the release valve that could let both banks pivot dovish again. Investment angle: Stagflation favors real assets, energy, and quality; a clean oil-driven disinflation would re-open the rate-cut trade.

JetBrains’ April 2026 AI Pulse found 46% named Claude Code most-loved (vs 19% Cursor, 9% Copilot), with Claude Code at 28% and Cursor 24% of primary-tool picks. Adoption runs 84–91% across surveys — but trust in AI accuracy fell to 29% (Stack Overflow, down 11 points). GitHub Copilot moved all plans to usage-based “GitHub AI Credits” (June 1), and Fable 5 hit GA in Copilot June 9.

So what: Adoption is near-universal yet trust is falling — developers use AI heavily while increasingly verifying its output, which favors tools with strong agentic guardrails and transparency. Investment angle: The accuracy-trust gap is the wedge for tools that emphasize verifiability, testing, and review over raw generation.

Enterprise Agents — 40% of Apps to Embed AI Agents by End-2026
Gartner projects 40% of enterprise apps will integrate task-specific AI agents by end-2026, up from less than 5% in 2025. MCP (Model Context Protocol) is becoming the standard for exposing tools to agents, with orchestration plus RAG/vector DBs as the dominant architecture; vendors include Microsoft, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Google, AWS, UiPath, LangChain, and CrewAI.

So what: Multi-agent orchestration is moving from experiment to default enterprise architecture in a single year, and MCP standardization is the connective tissue making it deployable. Investment angle: Agent orchestration, tool-connectivity (MCP), and vector/RAG infrastructure are the foundational layers of the enterprise-agent buildout.
