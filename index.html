2026 Iran War — Inspections Breakthrough June 24: Vance Says Iran to Let Inspectors Resume After “Good Day”; Tehran Disputes Timing
The verification deadlock eased — partly. After the June 21 US-Iran “road map” (Strait of Hormuz, frozen assets, end to operations in Lebanon), VP Vance said June 24 that Iran agreed to let international nuclear inspections resume after a “good day” of talks, and IAEA chief Grossi said inspectors will visit Iranian sites though the exact timing is “not essential.” But Tehran immediately pushed back: an Iranian diplomat said a visit can only come after a final deal, exposing the remaining gap. Trump says Iran is meeting US demands but warns Washington “will act if the deal fails”; Tehran says it is proceeding “with vigilance.” Tankers are now transiting the Strait of Hormuz, and the US is weighing redirecting frozen Iranian assets to Gulf states for reconstruction. The 60-day interim clock still governs.

So what: The deal moved from a road map to a tentative inspections concession — the durability question is now “before or after final deal,” not “whether.” Progress drove crude below $74. Investment angle: Energy tail hedges keep bleeding as the path firms; reconstruction/sanctions-relief plays activate only on a verified final settlement.

Trade Policy — Trump Rebuilds Tariff Engine on Section 301 After Supreme Court Struck Down IEEPA; 60 Countries Face 10–12.5%
After the Supreme Court ruled Feb 20 (Learning Resources v. Trump) that IEEPA does NOT authorize sweeping open-ended tariffs, the administration is quietly rebuilding the wall on firmer legal ground. A new June report proposes Section 301 forced-labor tariffs of 10–12.5% on ~60 countries — 10% on Canada, Mexico and the EU; 12.5% on China, Brazil, Japan and India (Bloomberg, June 22) — using authority with no statutory cap on level or duration. This stacks on the standing Section 232 50% metals tariffs (steel/aluminum/copper) and the extended 30% China rate. The shift from IEEPA to 301/232 makes the tariffs slower to deploy but far harder to overturn.

So what: Tariffs survived the courts by changing legal vehicle — a structural inflation tax that is widening, not disappearing, even as energy disinflates. Investment angle: Bullish domestic metals; bearish import-heavy retail, machinery, HVAC and construction inputs; reshoring beneficiaries in semis, pharma and medical equipment stay favored.
