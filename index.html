⚠️ War & Geopolitics
Russia-Ukraine — Largest Aerial Assault of 2026, but Russian Advance Stalls
Russia launched one of the largest aerial attacks of the full-scale war on June 2, firing 73 cruise, ballistic, and hypersonic missiles plus 656 attack drones across Ukraine. At least 22 killed (including 2 children) and 130 injured. Ukrainian air defenses intercepted 40 missiles and 602 drones. Despite this escalation, Russia’s ground advance has stalled — assessments show Russia at a territorial standstill in 2026, with Ukraine credited for steadily strengthening its UAV capabilities. Ukrainian forces struck the Ilsky oil refinery in Russia’s Krasnodar Krai (~500km from Ukrainian lines). European officials say Moscow’s aggression reflects increasing military and economic difficulties.

So what: The stall is the real story. Russia is compensating for ground failure with record aerial bombardment, but Ukraine’s drone-first doctrine is neutralizing Russia’s air advantage at 1/100th the cost per engagement. The Ilsky refinery strike at 500km range demonstrates Ukraine’s expanding deep-strike capability, which threatens Russia’s logistics and energy infrastructure — the asymmetric pressure that actually changes the strategic calculus. Investment angle: European defense spending (2.5%+ GDP) locked in for a decade. Rheinmetall, BAE, Leonardo remain structural longs. Drone/counter-drone companies (AeroVironment, Kratos) are the fastest-growing segment. Ukraine reconstruction ($500B+) activates on any ceasefire.

Investment angle: At $222 and $5.5T market cap, NVIDIA needs continued perfection. Ebola PHEIC Declared — 321 Cases in DRC, Spreading to Uganda
WHO declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) on May 17, 2026 for the Ebola outbreak in DRC and Uganda caused by the Bundibugyo virus. As of June 1: DRC reports 321 confirmed cases and 48 confirmed deaths; Uganda has 15 confirmed cases with 1 death. The 220 additional suspected cases in DRC suggest the true case count is significantly higher. This is only the seventh PHEIC for Ebola and the first for a non-Zaire strain.

So what: The PHEIC declaration is warranted — cross-border spread to Uganda confirms containment has failed in the initial phase. Bundibugyo virus is particularly challenging because existing vaccines (rVSV-ZEBOV) were designed for Zaire ebolavirus and may have reduced efficacy against this strain. The DRC’s Ituri province is also an active conflict zone, complicating response logistics. The PHEIC triggers international funding, but WHO budget was cut 9% for 2026–27. Investment angle: PHEIC activates funding for vaccine developers (Merck, J&J, Bavarian Nordic). If new vaccines needed, mRNA platform companies (Moderna, BioNTech) can rapidly design candidates. Diagnostic companies (Cepheid, Abbott) see near-term demand.
