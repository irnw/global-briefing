Brent Tops $100 for First Time Since May: Houthis Strike Two Saudi Tankers in the Red Sea; War Risk Widens Beyond Hormuz to Bab el-Mandeb
The conflict has jumped a chokepoint. On July 23, Iran-aligned Houthis struck two Saudi oil tankers — the Encelia and Layla — in the Red Sea, ~70–130 km off the Saudi coast in the corridor approaching the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, igniting a fire aboard the Encelia (crew safe) after it departed Yanbu. The Houthis had declared a maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia this week. Oil re-priced the widening war: Brent surged ~6–7% to close near $100.69–$101.10/bbl — crossing $100 for the first time since May 26. The market is now pricing a two-chokepoint supply risk (Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb), with analysts warning of a path toward the 2022 high of ~$128 if attacks on Gulf infrastructure persist.

So what: The threat map just doubled — Red Sea transit is now a live front, not a peripheral one. Investment angle: VLCC day-rates, war-risk insurance premia, tanker re-routing (Cape of Good Hope) and energy stay bid on every Red Sea/Hormuz headline; a sustained closure of either strait resets the global oil complex higher.

A statutory tariff cliff hits today. The Section 122 15% global surcharge — imposed after the Supreme Court's Feb 20 ruling that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs — expires July 24, a hard limit only Congress can extend.
