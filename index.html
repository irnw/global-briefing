⚠️ War & Geopolitics
Hormuz Is Two-Thirds Recovered and That Is the Whole Story — Goldman Puts Gulf Crude Exports at 15–16 mb/d Against a 5–6 mb/d March Trough
The 2026 Iran war — opened by the US and Israel on 28 February 2026, killing Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei in the first salvo — produced a 7–8 April ceasefire that collapsed in July after Iran struck three commercial vessels running the restrictions. The live variable is not the front line, it is the Strait of Hormuz, which carried ~20% of global oil supply pre-war. Goldman Sachs puts Persian Gulf crude exports back at 15–16 million b/d, roughly two-thirds of pre-war volume, against a 5–6 mb/d trough in March. Tehran and Oman agreed route coordinates through the Strait on 5 August; attacks on shipping since then have repeatedly stalled a formal reopening. The US imposed its eighth round of Iran sanctions this year in early August. On 21 August, Iran's president signalled Tehran wants the war ended soon — oil barely moved on it.

So what: The market has already priced the recovery, which is why a presidential peace signal on 21 August moved Brent almost nothing. That asymmetry is the trade: the remaining 7–8 mb/d gap is worth far more to the downside on a real reopening than the upside on another tanker strike. Positioning for escalation here is late. The EIA does not expect Middle East production back near pre-conflict levels until early 2027 — so this is a two-quarter normalisation trade, not a two-week one. Watch bunker rates and Gulf war-risk insurance premia rather than headlines; they lead the physical reopening by weeks.

Ukraine: Budanov Floats September Trilateral Talks — But Lavrov Ruled Out a Frontline Freeze Two Weeks Earlier
Trilateral US–Ukraine–Russia negotiations have been suspended since February 2026, when the Iran war broke out. On 27 August, Presidential Office head Kyrylo Budanov said talks could resume as early as September, with proposals covering a ceasefire and security guarantees; Zelenskyy confirmed on 12 August that Kyiv had handed Washington new proposals to end the four-and-a-half-year war. Against that: on 14 August Foreign Minister Lavrov explicitly rejected any ceasefire that freezes the current front line, and on 21 August Moscow said it was open to "new ideas" only insofar as they match Putin's existing goals — which still include all of Donbas, including Ukrainian-held territory. Investment angle: Do not underwrite European reconstruction, Ukrainian grain-corridor normalisation or EU defence-spending mean-reversion off a September talks headline. The four-year base rate on announced resumptions of this process is poor — US mediation since early 2025 has produced only Black Sea navigation limits, energy-infrastructure restraint and a three-day ceasefire. The Chokepoint Doctrine: Venezuela Set the Precedent, Hormuz Proved the Mechanism, Taiwan Is the Position Being Priced
In early January 2026 the US military removed Venezuelan president Nicolás Maduro from Caracas, with Trump pledging to "run" Venezuela himself. CSIS, TIME and CNBC have all since run the same analysis: the episode strengthens the proposition that great powers act first and litigate afterwards. Analysts expect Beijing to test Taipei in 2026 with expanded naval exercises or limited blockades rather than invasion, with miscalculation risk highest if China misreads the US or allied response function. The Foreign Affairs Forum's 14 August framing is the useful one: the primary battlegrounds have migrated to chokepoints, supply chains and dual-use technology rather than force-on-force. Elsewhere, Sudan's war continues at scale — 12 million displaced, up to 33.7 million needing food aid, with an expected RSF siege of El Obeid trapping 500,000 people as the next flashpoint.

So what: Three chokepoint events in eight months — Caracas, Hormuz, and Taiwan Strait exercises — is a pattern, not a coincidence, and it reprices a specific risk: concentrated single-point physical dependency. Investment angle: The durable trade is not defence primes, which are already bid; it is the duplication layer — second-source foundry capacity outside Taiwan, non-Hormuz pipeline and LNG routing, rare-earth processing outside China, and the marine-insurance and route-optimisation software that prices all of it. Every one of those was a cost centre in 2023 and is a strategic budget line in 2026. Note the honest caveat: a limited blockade is far more likely than an invasion, and the two have completely different investment implications — a blockade is a logistics and insurance event, an invasion is a global recession.

The Fed Is Stuck: Core PCE at 3.4%, Rates Held at 3.65%, and the July Minutes Name AI Capex as an Inflation Source
The FOMC held the interest rate on reserve balances at 3.65%, effective 30 July 2026. Core PCE inflation is 3.4% — both headline and core higher than a year ago. The minutes attribute this to three named forces: past tariff increases, higher energy and input costs from the Middle East conflict, and — notably — the demand surge from the AI buildout. On tariffs, the effective rate implied by customs duties went from 2.1% to an estimated 11.7% as of January 2026, and the best current evidence puts consumer pass-through above 50%. Stanford SIEPR frames the 2026 watch-list as rates, a weakening job market, AI-firm valuations, affordability (healthcare, electricity) and limited fiscal space.

So what: This is the genuinely uncomfortable configuration and most commentary is soft on it. A central bank facing above-target inflation and a weakening labour market has no clean move — cutting feeds an inflation impulse it has already identified, holding accelerates labour deterioration. What makes 2026 different from a standard stagflation setup is the third driver: the Fed has now formally named AI capex as an inflationary force. That is a policy statement with teeth. Investment angle: If AI buildout demand is contributing to core inflation, then the Fed's reaction function has become partially endogenous to hyperscaler capex. That is a channel almost nothing in the market is priced for: a scenario where strong AI capex delays rate cuts, which compresses the discount-rate tailwind that AI equities depend on. Rate-cut-dependent long-duration positions and AI capex beta are far more correlated than they look.

China Grew 5% in Q1 Against a 4.8% Consensus — But the Composition Is Still Wrong
China's GDP expanded 5% in Q1 2026, beating the 4.8% consensus and hitting the leadership's 4.5–5% target. Goldman Sachs projects 4.8% for full-year 2026, driven by exports and a fading property drag. Exports proved resilient to higher US tariffs, with real inflation-adjusted growth around 8% in 2025, largely via emerging markets, ASEAN and the EU rather than the US, and increasingly in higher-value equipment and automobiles. Fiscal support runs through a general budget deficit at 4% of GDP and RMB 1.3 trillion of ultra-long special treasury bonds. Bruegel's assessment is blunt: stimulus without rebalancing — growth still leans on manufacturing and exports while consumption stays weak.

So what: The headline beat is real and the structure underneath it is unchanged, which is the thing to hold both of at once. A country that answers weak domestic demand by exporting more manufactured goods into the EU and ASEAN is exporting its deflation. That is the mechanism to watch, not the GDP print. Investment angle: This is the most underpriced trade-policy risk of H2 2026 — EU and ASEAN trade defence measures against Chinese industrial goods, particularly autos, capital equipment and solar. Chinese export diversification away from US tariffs has mechanically raised the political temperature in exactly the markets absorbing that volume. European industrials with China import competition are carrying a policy option they are not being paid for; the same dynamic makes ASEAN local-content rules a live 2027 risk.

The policy chronology is now clear. The 25% revenue-share rule is the tell: a government that structures a tax on a trade flow expects the flow to exist. It does not.

🩺 Health
DRC Bundibugyo Ebola: 5,290 Cases, 2,516 Deaths — WHO Says It Is On Track to Surpass the 2014 West Africa Epidemic
As of 19 August, the DRC's Bundibugyo virus outbreak has 5,290 confirmed cases and 2,516 deaths — a ~47.6% case fatality rate. It has reached six provinces faster than any prior Ebola event on record, and WHO now says it is on track to surpass the 2014 West Africa epidemic. Countermeasures are moving but late: the first-ever Bundibugyo species-specific candidate vaccine entered trials in the UK on 24 July, with a second starting in Canada, and the International Coordinating Group released 70,000 doses of Ervebo on 18 August, split between a Phase 3 trial and frontline worker protection.

So what: The critical detail is the species. Ervebo is licensed against Zaire ebolavirus; this is Bundibugyo. Releasing 70,000 Ervebo doses into a Bundibugyo outbreak is a deployment under uncertainty, and the fact that the species-specific vaccine only entered Phase 1 five weeks ago tells you there is no licensed tool for this pathogen. Six provinces at 47.6% CFR with no matched vaccine is a genuine international spread risk, not a regional health story. Investment angle: The structural gap is not vaccine discovery, it is multivalent filovirus coverage and surge manufacturing — the world built a single-species answer to a multi-species genus and is now paying for it. Watch for emergency-use pathway decisions and any WHO PHEIC determination; both are step-function catalysts for filovirus platform developers and for African CDC procurement budgets.

Eight Years Into the AI Drug Discovery Boom, Zero Approvals in Any Major Market
The single most important sentence in pharma this month, and it is a negative: nearly eight years into the AI drug discovery boom, no medicine discovered or designed with AI has been approved by a regulator in any major market. What the field has accumulated is capital, trial starts, and a thin first layer of human data. Meanwhile conventional pipelines delivered: J&J won a second US approval for Imaavy (nipocalimab) in warm autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (12+); BMS's Zenbexus (iberdomide) was approved for multiple myeloma after at least one prior line. In obesity, Hanmi licensed HM17321 (UCN2) exclusively to Roche's Genentech, and Eli Lilly filed six new lawsuits against black-market sales of retatrutide.

So what: Eight years and zero approvals is not a timing complaint — it is roughly the full duration of a discovery-to-approval cycle, which means the first cohort has had its chance. The charitable read is that AI-originated assets entered clinic from ~2020 and Phase 3 readouts are genuinely still pending; the uncharitable read is that the bottleneck was never molecule generation, it was clinical failure in Phase 2, and AI does not address that. The evidence to date cannot distinguish between these, and anyone telling you it can is selling something. Investment angle: Price AI-discovery platforms on Phase 2 success rate versus the industry base rate, not on candidate count, pipeline breadth or partnership announcements. Lilly's six retatrutide lawsuits are the more immediately investable signal: grey-market demand at that intensity for a pre-approval asset is a demand datapoint no survey can replicate.

🌐 Emerging World Trends
1
The Constraint Migrated From Compute to Electrons
Every serious 2026 datapoint points the same way: datacentre electricity demand +17% in 2025, consumption doubling by 2030, AI-specific power tripling, AI workloads 15%→40% of datacentre load by 2030, four SMR startups reaching criticality, a $1.05bn Series A for grid orchestration, and TSMC seeing 2nm demand exceed 3nm before ramp — that last one is a power-efficiency purchase, not a performance one. The Fed has now formally named the AI buildout as a contributor to core inflation at 3.4%. The scarce resource in AI is no longer GPUs; it is interconnection capacity, firm baseload, and the permitting timeline to get either. This reframes the entire capex stack: the marginal dollar of AI investment increasingly buys power infrastructure, not silicon.
2
Open Weights Went Chinese, and the West Has Not Priced It
Per Hugging Face's 14 August state-of-open-models report: Alibaba's Qwen passed 3 billion cumulative downloads against Google's 418 million and Meta's 227 million — roughly 7x the two American open-weight leaders combined. Qwen has 460+ open-sourced models spawning 300,000+ derivatives. In August alone Alibaba shipped Qwen3.8-Max at 2.4 trillion parameters with a 1m-token context window (claimed competitive with Anthropic's frontier), then Qwen3.8-Flash with open weights. DeepSeek V4-Pro went to full production on 12 August after a four-month preview. The strategic consequence is not model quality, it is defaults: the global developer ecosystem's fine-tuning base, tooling and documentation are being built on Chinese weights, and that dependency compounds silently and is very difficult to unwind.
3
Chokepoint Politics Replaces Force-on-Force
Caracas in January, Hormuz through the spring and summer, Taiwan Strait exercises in August. The pattern is coherent: great powers now contest chokepoints, supply chains and dual-use technology rather than territory. Hormuz carried ~20% of global oil and exports remain 7–8 mb/d below pre-war; the Venezuela precedent normalised acting first and litigating later; the expected Taiwan scenario is a limited blockade, not an invasion. The corporate consequence, and it is already visible in capital budgets: single-point physical dependency has become a board-level risk with a quantified cost, which is why duplication spending — second-source foundries, alternative routing, domestic rare-earth processing — moved from cost centre to strategic line item in a single fiscal cycle.
4
Regulation Split Into Transparency Now, Capability Later
The EU began enforcing the AI Act on 2 August 2026 via the AI Office and national authorities, with new transparency rules live the same day — chatbots must disclose they are not human, and deepfakes must be labelled. But under the Digital Omnibus provisionally agreed 7 May 2026, the high-risk Annex III deadline slipped from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027. Meanwhile Singapore's IMDA launched the world's first Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI in January, updated in May, with an AI Tester Accreditation Programme due Q3 2026 — the first in Asia. Indonesia is finalising a Presidential Regulation plus Ministry of Law Regulation No. 5/2026 on IP digitalisation. The pattern across all three jurisdictions is identical: enforce disclosure obligations immediately because they are cheap, defer capability obligations because they are contested. Compliance spend in the next 18 months goes to labelling, provenance and audit trails — not to model safety.
5
Capital Concentration Is Now the Defining Feature of Venture
US and Canadian startup funding hit $392bn in H1 2026 alone, records shattered, AI driving nearly all of it. But the structure is the story: more capital chasing fewer breakout deals, faster, at higher valuations, against tighter theses. August alone saw Databricks $5bn at $190bn, Fireworks AI $1.505bn Series D, Together AI $800m Series C, Firmus $2bn at $10.5bn, and Emerald AI $150m at $1.05bn — at Series A. Simultaneously, VCs expect 2026 to be the year the market weeds out young AI startups, with thin-margin companies most at risk. Both halves are happening at once, and that is the point: a barbell, not a boom. Founding-team career expertise remains the most heavily weighted seed and Series A factor — in a market this concentrated, credentialism is the rationing mechanism, which systematically underweights non-obvious founders and creates the arbitrage for anyone willing to underwrite them.

Stablecoins Crossed From Crypto Instrument to Payments Infrastructure — Seven Major Economies Now Regulate Them as Payment Instruments
In 2026 stablecoins entered the regulatory mainstream across seven major economies — US, EU, UK, Singapore, Hong Kong, UAE and Japan — all of which now mandate full reserve backing, licensed issuers and guaranteed redemption rights, and all of which now treat stablecoins as regulated payment instruments rather than crypto assets. In the US, banks, credit unions and specially licensed non-bank issuers can issue under OCC oversight.

So what: The reclassification is the whole event and it is being underrated because it is legal rather than technological. "Payment instrument" and "crypto asset" are different regulatory objects with different capital treatment, different accounting, and critically, different permission for a regulated bank to touch them. Full reserve backing plus guaranteed redemption is functionally a narrow bank — which means the economics are float income on the reserves, and that makes stablecoin issuer profitability a direct short on the front end of the rate curve. At a 3.65% policy rate the model is excellent; at 1.5% it is not a business. Investment angle: Underwrite stablecoin issuers as leveraged plays on short rates staying elevated, not as payments-volume growth stories. Almost nobody models them this way, and the correlation will be discovered abruptly during the first meaningful cutting cycle.
