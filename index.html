80% of Enterprise Apps Now Embed an Agent — and 88–89% of Agent Pilots Never Reach Production. Adoption Is Racing Ahead of the Success Rate.
88–89% of agent pilots never reach production (Deloitte, HCLTech, Nasuni), and MIT's Project NANDA found 95% of genAI pilots produce no measurable business impact. Against that, 80% of enterprise apps shipped or updated in Q1 2026 embed at least one AI agent (Gartner), up from 33% in 2024. Roughly 74% of the largest enterprises run AI in production, but half still cannot consistently measure ROI (Forbes, Aug 6, 2026). Forrester's root-cause breakdown: 41% unclear success criteria, 33% insufficient tool or data access, 26% evaluation coverage drift. Only 21% have mature agent governance; 52% cite data quality as the top blocker. Gartner projects >40% of agentic AI projects at risk of cancellation by 2027, with enterprises already rolling back deployed agents.

So what: Read the failure taxonomy carefully, because it is not what the headline implies. 41% unclear success criteria and 33% insufficient data access are organisational failures, not model failures — which means the deployment gap is largely addressable and largely not a technology problem. That is why both frontier labs shipped enterprise governance features the same week. Investment angle: Evaluation, observability and success-criteria tooling sit directly on the largest identified failure mode, and the buyer already has budget allocated to the failing pilots.

Usage Is Rising While Trust Collapses: 84% Use AI Tools, but Trust in Accuracy Fell 40% → 29% and Only 3% Are "Highly Trusting"
Stack Overflow's most recent published survey: 84% using or planning to use AI (up from 76%), 51% of professional developers using it daily — but trust in accuracy fell from 40% to 29%, 46% actively distrust AI output, and only 3% are "highly trusting." The top frustration, cited by 66%: "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite." 45% say debugging AI-generated code takes longer than writing it manually. DORA respondents report a median 2 hours per day of AI-assisted work. Sourcing note: these are 2025-survey figures. The 2026 survey opened Jun 23, 2026 and results were not published as of Aug 28 — at least one outlet has republished the 2025 numbers under a 2026 headline.

So what: "Almost right, but not quite" is the precise economic problem, because a nearly-correct answer transfers the cost from writing to verification without reducing it — which is exactly the METR result arriving from a different direction. Rising usage alongside falling trust is not contradictory; it describes a tool people feel obliged to use and unable to rely on. Investment angle: The 45% debugging figure is the most direct statement of where the money is: anything that makes AI output verifiable rather than merely plausible is selling into a measured, quantified pain point.
