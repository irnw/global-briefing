13 Nov Update: Abu Dhabi Ends With a Prisoner-Swap Deal and No Breakthrough — Kushner: “Very Tough but Productive”; Envoys Head to Moscow, Then Dublin; Four Tankers Hit in Hormuz in Two Days (Iran: Mines, “Restricted Areas”); White House Keeps the Blockade
Ukraine (Al Jazeera, Forbes, Moscow Times, 11–12 Nov): the two-day round closed on 11 Nov with agreement in principle on a large-scale prisoner exchange — Zelensky said proposals ranged from 1,000-for-1,000 to 1,500-for-1,500 (the record is 1,000-for-1,000 in May 2025); TASS says the swap should happen in 2–3 weeks. Kushner called it “a very tough meeting but a productive one,” claimed “progress” on a post-war security framework and said territory will be the hardest issue. Positions did not move: Medinsky said “every inch of Russian land will be returned to Russia”; Lavrov rejected “yet another truce” without a settlement; Kyiv wants a ceasefire on the current line and US guarantees of at least 20 years. Next: Witkoff and Kushner brief Putin in Moscow, then Zelensky in Dublin; no date for a next trilateral round. Ukraine’s Arakhamia wants Europe at the table and cites “good momentum” before year-end. On the ground, Russia hit 10 regions and 5 major substations on 10 Nov (Reuters via Forbes); Ukrainian drones set fire to the Ust-Luga Baltic oil-export port. Gulf (Reuters/Yahoo, Gulf Today, Fox, 11–12 Nov): four tankers were hit in two days — the IRGC Navy says two supertankers (over 4M barrels combined) were disabled by a “violent explosion” on 11 Nov, and Iran’s army says two more hit mines on 12 Nov after entering “restricted areas”; Reuters could not verify, and the US military had said the strait was cleared of mines. The White House says the naval blockade and Operations Economic Outcast and Iron Gate continue; Iran is now demanding war-damage compensation at COP13; Saudi air defences downed two Houthi missiles aimed at Mecca. So what: a prisoner swap is the classic consolation prize when talks stall — the real test is whether the Moscow/Dublin shuttle produces a date for round two. In the Gulf, the mine claim is the new risk: if Iran can make transit unsafe without firing openly, the US “cleared the mines” assurance loses value and insurers, not navies, decide the flow. Gap: exact prisoner numbers not finalised; tanker names, flags and casualties not reported; Reuters/AP article text partly unavailable.
